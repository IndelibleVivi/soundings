# Proactive inquiry and reference choice

Use this reference when a substantial product, form, interaction, visual, architecture, or implementation choice is still open, or when comparing several references. Read it before committing substantial work, not after the solution has already organized the implementation. Stay within the current task's authority and reading boundaries. For complete walkthroughs, read [proactive inquiry examples](proactive-inquiry-examples.md) only when multiple-reference judgment or repeated rejection makes detailed diagnosis useful.

## Recognize the open choice

Inspect relevant outside possibilities when an unresolved choice could be changed by an implemented system, an observable interaction, a visual or experiential work, a mechanism, or an adoption condition. The open choice is enough reason to look; the person does not need to request research, name a target, or prove that an outside system would improve the work. A clear goal and confidence in a familiar solution are not evidence that the solution space is settled. Existing authorization still decides whether and how outside material can be inspected.

Prior references that still answer the live question can be reused. Look outward again when the open choice has changed, when a prior source did not expose the deciding medium or condition, or when implementation reveals a new dependency. The unit is the current open choice, not the age of the repository.

Use the current decision's consequence and uncertainty to bound the work. A local reversible choice rarely needs a broad survey; a commitment that will organize substantial architecture, content, or interaction may need direct observation of several genuinely different possibilities. No fixed reference quota or investigation sequence follows from this method.

Proceed directly only when the scope is settled and adequate applicable evidence is already present: a clear small repair, a faithful implementation of a genuinely settled choice, or supplied-material synthesis within its stated boundary. A feature may be substantial without requiring discovery when its behavior and source of truth are fixed. Conversely, an ordinary-looking visual or interaction change becomes inquiry when the desired effect, relationship, or implementation boundary remains open.

## Fit the material to the use

- **Direction or possibility:** inspect enough relevant works to perceive relationships, not merely collect labels or feature claims.
- **Experience:** use the actual medium, sequence, interaction, recording, or representative run. A description or still image may establish a claim about a timed or interactive work without establishing its experience.
- **Mechanism:** inspect the relevant source, dataflow, tests, or a probe that exposes behavior.
- **Adoption:** check the local interface, state ownership, maintenance burden, version, and license conditions that would make the mechanism suitable here.
- **Synthesis:** existing references may be sufficient. Discovery does not require an already-known gap when the assignment still seeks possibilities.

Keep the source claim, direct observation, inference, and local transfer distinct. "The demonstration behaves this way", "this is why it works in that setting", and "it would produce this effect here" are different claims. No amount of README detail establishes an experiential assessment that was not observed.

## Compare across the live problem

Give every named target proportionate direct coverage. Triage candidates discovered along the way, but do not let a long result list replace examination of the target the person actually named.

Organize comparison around a small set of shared questions, scenarios, or effects. This exposes what candidates actually disagree about; parallel per-repository summaries often hide it.

- **Competing alternatives** solve the same local question under incompatible conditions.
- **Complementary contributions** supply different parts of a coherent solution without competing for the same responsibility.
- **Incompatible preconditions or state ownership** can make two attractive mechanisms unsafe to combine.
- **Promising unused clues** matter when they suggest a relationship or observation the current direction has not tested.

Follow dependencies, forks, citations, and related work when they change the comparison. A fork or mirror is not automatically an independent direction; it may inherit the same assumptions or source. Use adjacent fields or independent vocabularies when results keep circling one family of concepts. There is no fixed top-three cutoff and no universal ladder from overview to deep read. Depth follows the question and the consequence of being wrong.

## Locate local progress and reference coverage

Distinguish local project state from what was actually checked outside it:

- **Local implemented, not accepted:** code, a build, or a working prototype exists, but the owner has not accepted its result. Presence is not product progress.
- **Local accepted baseline:** accepted identity, behavior, constraints, or parts of an implementation that later work must preserve unless evidence justifies reopening them. Open dimensions can coexist with this baseline; a mature project may add a new domain without reopening what already works.
- **Local open dimension:** a current choice not yet settled. It is the discovery unit when substantial.
- **Local observed result:** a designated run, fixture, probe, or feedback event. It establishes one result, not acceptance of the wider product.
- **Reference observed:** the outside material was directly inspected in the relevant medium. A description, search result, repository name, cited claim, or generated summary is not the same.
- **Reference not yet observed:** discovered or mentioned material that may matter but has not been covered. Keep it as a clue or a gap rather than counting it as evidence.

Preserve which local observation, source claim, and inference support a judgment without forcing them into an enum or registry. A source may be implemented and mature in its own setting and still be only a candidate relationship here; another source may contribute an interaction without supplying a dependency. A mature accepted baseline is a reason to compare preservation and integration conditions, not a reason to skip a new open domain.

A blank project with genuinely open direction needs coherent candidates rather than a claim that nothing can be chosen. A blank project with a settled direction needs faithful depth in that direction. A mature project can contain a newly open domain; inspect references for that domain while preserving accepted parts that the new choice does not threaten. A larger proposal can be justified, but it is not an automatic rewrite.

After repeated rejection, inspect the actual failed outputs together with the stated intent and the reference relationship. Then distinguish whether the problem lies in:

- interpretation of the goal;
- the interaction or composition relationship;
- execution quality;
- candidate fidelity, where the thing shown did not expose the intended effect.

Choose the next attempt to discriminate among those explanations. "Try again with more polish" is not a diagnosis, and one rejection does not justify removing an accepted quality from every later candidate. Read the [constructed rejection walkthrough](proactive-inquiry-examples.md#constructed-example-repeated-rejection) when the failed output, tempting alternative, and accepted baseline all need to be reconciled.

## Carry the inquiry back

When the material has answered the live question, return the relationship, condition, or counterevidence to the actual work. Read [Returning findings to the work](returning-to-the-work.md) when a handoff, changed question, or affected implementation needs the full receiver method. Use [evidence and stopping](evidence-and-stopping.md) to choose whether to broaden, deepen, probe, build, execute, or stop.
