# Evidence and stopping

Use this reference when Search needs to decide where to look, how far to go, or whether the inquiry has become sufficient.

## Match evidence to the question

- Current platform, API, provider, policy, law, price, or product capability: prefer current primary documentation and observed runtime behavior.
- How a mechanism works: inspect the maintained implementation or reproduce the behavior when practical.
- What a person experiences: inspect the actual artifact, flow, recording, export, or representative sample rather than relying only on feature claims.
- A creative possibility: use relevant works, demonstrations, practices, and adjacent domains; explain the transferable relationship instead of citing novelty alone.
- A user preference or authorial choice: rely on the current conversation and scoped evidence. Outside sources cannot decide it.

Search breadth and depth are independent. A narrow factual question may require deep source tracing; an open direction may need only enough range to reveal one genuinely distinct possibility.

## Follow conditions and disagreements

Treat a claim together with the conditions under which it holds. If a feature page, plan table, and observed output disagree, first align their dates, versions, plans, operation types, and audiences. A claim about a preview is not automatically a claim about an exported file. Preserve a real unresolved conflict when aligning those conditions does not explain it.

Trace a decisive statement to its origin where feasible. Several engines returning one page provide several discovery paths, not several independent observations. Different pages may repeat one announcement. Prefer the source that can establish the claim; do not demand a second source when one authoritative specification settles it, or treat a promotional claim as observed behavior.

A useful synthesis states what the disagreement changes. For example, if one export route supports transparent backgrounds while another does not, the result is conditional on the route; averaging the sources into “partial support” obscures the actionable distinction.

When the consequence matters to a person, inspect the delivered artifact or interaction. The decisive criterion may be missing from the original query. Update the inquiry when an observation exposes it, rather than only re-ranking candidates against the initial wording.

## Read enough to support the conclusion

Expand a source when the answer depends on an exception, a clipped passage, a table heading or note, a precise version, a conflict, or an inference stronger than the excerpt supports. A link makes checking possible; it does not establish that checking happened. If the full source is inaccessible, weaken the conclusion and name the coverage limit.

Read around decisive matches, including adjacent conditions and referenced notes. Keep literal material, extraction, and model-generated summaries distinguishable. An exact quotation is not a guarantee that all relevant context survived selection.

Preserve useful room for discovery: strong filtering against the first question can discard the observation that would improve that question. Follow an unused detail when its mechanism or consequence is relevant to the goal, and explain that relevance. Do not turn every unrelated snippet into a new research branch.

## Keep the query open to disconfirmation

Frame a question tightly enough to be answerable without preselecting the result. “How do tools keep generated variation editable?” is useful. “Find a node editor proving we need nodes” is not.

When the current solution was introduced by the agent and governs substantial work, include evidence that could change or disconfirm it. Do not use research only to decorate a path already chosen.

## Choose the next useful action

At any point, choose the action that can resolve the live uncertainty:

- **Broaden:** continue discovery for new relevant families when the current results repeat one vocabulary, inherit the same assumptions, or leave a named target uncovered.
- **Deepen:** inspect the shared decisive distinction when several candidates appear competitive or complementary for different reasons.
- **Probe local fit:** observe state, interface, version, license, performance, or integration conditions when the source works but adoption remains uncertain.
- **Inspect or build the medium:** use the actual interaction, timed sequence, rendered artifact, or representative candidate when experience is the open question.
- **Execute:** change, implement, or deliver the authorized work once the evidence supports it and no unresolved owner choice obstructs the next action.
- **Stop:** finish the commission when its requested result and important counterevidence are adequately supported, or when another source would add links without changing the answer.

These are choices, not mandatory stages or a quality score. Move directly to the action that resolves the uncertainty, and return to discovery only when new evidence or an open choice makes it useful.

## Stop according to the intent

- **Verify:** complete when the decision-relevant claim has adequate current support or the remaining uncertainty is explicit and cannot be resolved proportionately.
- **Compare:** complete when the differences that could plausibly change this choice are covered. Do not enumerate every vendor or implementation.
- **Discover:** complete when the concrete directions and decisive distinctions the commission requested are covered, or the results have stopped changing the possibility space. One promising direction may justify designing or making it rather than searching endlessly.

These are stopping rules for inquiry actions, not permission to shrink the commission. A broad discovery request may require several developed directions; a complete research request may still need synthesis after retrieval ends. Judge completion against what the user asked to learn or receive, including important counterexamples and open questions. An experiment design can be the requested deliverable; a proposal is incomplete only when the authorized experiment, artifact, or implementation is still due. Do not hand back “research this next” when that research is already authorized and feasible.

Continue when new evidence is still changing the question or the user explicitly requested broad exploration. Stop when investigation adds links but no longer changes facts, candidates, criteria, or the next useful action.

Account for the work needed to reach a usable result: provider calls and cost, waiting, returned material, repeat reads, user effort, and likely rework. Do not collapse these into an invented score. Research effort, per-response payload, and authorized retention need separate controls. A small response that forces many follow-up reads may cost more overall; a deep investigation may still deserve a short final answer.

## Name unavailable coverage honestly

If an important channel, source, version, artifact, or observed output is unavailable, state the limitation. Do not turn “not found” into “does not exist,” or an inaccessible sample into a confident experience claim.
