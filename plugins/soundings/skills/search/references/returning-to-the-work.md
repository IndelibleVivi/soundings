# Returning findings to the work

Use this reference when findings from a handoff, earlier inquiry, or external search must affect an ongoing project rather than remain a standalone report. This is the owner of receiver-side continuation. Ordinary answers do not need the full method.

## Recover the three layers

Do not treat the requested outcome, the current explanation, and the narrower research question as interchangeable:

- **Original desired outcome:** what the person is trying to make true, the use scene it serves, and the qualities and authority that bound delivery.
- **Current explanation:** the local interpretation or mechanism being tried, and the evidence that supports or challenges it.
- **Research or inquiry question:** the narrower uncertainty that external work was meant to resolve.

Returned material may answer the question, overturn the explanation, or reveal that the question itself was the wrong one. Recover the original outcome before reorganizing the results around the live problem. Do not inherit a distant report's framing merely because it is detailed. Do not change a research-only commission into implementation or an authorized larger commission into a report-only stopping point.

## Form a local judgment

Reconcile returned evidence with local artifacts, observed behavior, accepted choices, constraints, and unresolved dimensions. Distinguish support from conflict, and carry source conditions into the comparison. Form your own explanation, or keep competing explanations until a useful observation can distinguish them.

The finding does not automatically transfer. Ask what actual object, state boundary, or behavior must change for the requested effect, and whether the local conditions support the relationship that appeared outside. Preserve accepted parts and important counterevidence; do not combine attractive mechanisms whose state ownership or preconditions conflict.

If another source or observation is still needed, use [evidence and stopping](evidence-and-stopping.md) to decide whether to broaden, deepen, probe, build, or stop. If the needed method is supplied-material synthesis, use Study's own method rather than turning a research handoff into a new brief.

## Carry the relationship through

When the commission authorizes delivery, carry the learned relationship through the affected plan, specification, task order, code, content, and acceptance criteria. Update dependencies whose premises changed, and retire superseded active paths within authority. A feature name or a repaired report is not enough when the relationship it represents still fails in the local work.

Execute when the evidence supports the authorized change. Inspect or build the actual medium when its experiential effect is the unresolved question. A probe remains evidence rather than automatic product commitment. If installation, external services, private data, deployment, or another restricted action becomes necessary, stop at that permission boundary and report the exact next action.

## Return to the initiating scene

Revisit the original use scene and check what can now be understood or done that could not before. Locate any remaining failure rather than treating it as one undifferentiated research miss:

- **Source interpretation:** the source itself was misread or its qualification was lost.
- **Local fit:** the mechanism does not hold under this project's conditions.
- **Translation:** the relationship did not survive the local medium or interaction.
- **Implementation:** the intended behavior is not present or correct in the artifact.
- **Problem frame:** the inquiry answered a different question from the one the original outcome needed.

Repair the affected layer and its dependents. A useful explanation can complete a research commission; when the requested result at that layer is an experiment design, that design can be the deliverable. Stopping at a proposal is incomplete only when the authorized outcome still requires the experiment, artifact, or implementation now.

## Place each finding where it has force

| Finding | Effect on the work |
| --- | --- |
| Verified fact or constraint | Correct the relevant premise, decision, or implementation. Reopen only descendants that depend on it. |
| Mechanism | State why it works and whether the local conditions match. Adopt, adapt, compose, or probe the mechanism without importing its whole surrounding system by default. |
| Experience or work | Keep the source perceptible and identify the relationship worth pursuing. Let the real deliverable test whether that quality survived the translation. |
| Problem-changing discovery | Explain what earlier interpretation changed, which settled choices remain intact, and whether a new owner decision is actually needed. |
| Negative or insufficient result | Close the route, retain the current direction with stronger grounds, or preserve a bounded unknown. Do not invent a feature to justify the research. |

## Preserve source, inference, and transfer separately

A source proves what exists or happened in its own context. It does not prove that the same mechanism belongs here. State the transfer conditions and costs. A source can be useful even when none of its code, dependencies, or product structure should be adopted. An implemented result outside the project is not local acceptance, and local implementation is not acceptance either.

## Prefer a probe when observation is the missing evidence

Documentation may establish availability without establishing fit. If the user has authorized implementation or experimentation, make a probe representative enough to expose the deciding behavior. Do not optimize it for technical convenience while hiding the experience under evaluation.

A probe is evidence, not automatic product commitment. Retire it when it disproves the hypothesis; do not manufacture a requirement to protect sunk work.

## Resume rather than restart

Return findings to the active task and its existing decision record. Preserve a useful unused clue and a condition that would reopen the judgment only when the project already needs continuity. Do not create a separate research plan, duplicate brief, second ledger, or new approval round unless the work genuinely needs a durable artifact or an unowned decision has appeared.
