# Returning findings to the work

Use this reference when external inquiry must change an ongoing project rather than end as a standalone answer.

## Place each finding where it has force

| Finding | Effect on the work |
| --- | --- |
| Verified fact or constraint | Correct the relevant premise, decision, or implementation. Reopen only descendants that depend on it. |
| Mechanism | State why it works and whether the local conditions match. Adopt, adapt, compose, or probe the mechanism without importing its whole surrounding system by default. |
| Experience or work | Keep the source perceptible and identify the relationship worth pursuing. Let the real deliverable test whether that quality survived the translation. |
| Problem-changing discovery | Explain what earlier interpretation changed, which settled choices remain intact, and whether a new owner decision is actually needed. |
| Negative or insufficient result | Close the route, retain the current direction with stronger grounds, or preserve a bounded unknown. Do not invent a feature to justify the research. |

## Preserve source, inference, and transfer separately

A source proves what exists or happened in its own context. It does not prove that the same mechanism belongs here. State the transfer conditions and costs. A source can be useful even when none of its code, dependencies, or product structure should be adopted.

## Prefer a probe when observation is the missing evidence

Documentation may establish availability without establishing fit. If the user has authorized implementation or experimentation, make a probe representative enough to expose the deciding behavior. Do not optimize it for technical convenience while hiding the experience under evaluation.

A probe is evidence, not automatic product commitment. Retire it when it disproves the hypothesis; do not manufacture a requirement to protect sunk work.

## Resume rather than restart

Return findings to the active task and its existing decision record. Do not create a separate research plan, duplicate brief, or new approval round unless the work genuinely needs a durable artifact or an unowned decision has appeared.
