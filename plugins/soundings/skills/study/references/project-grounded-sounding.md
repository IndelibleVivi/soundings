# Project-grounded sounding

Use this reference when the material is an active project and the person asks what is worth pursuing, what the work has not yet understood, or how to continue without supplying a narrower research question.

## Find the question inside the work

Recover the project's accepted outcome, current artifacts or behavior, settled choices, and live proposals. Look for unresolved relationships, unexplained failures, unused evidence, or a gap between what the project claims and what people can actually do with it.

Consider only a few candidate questions. Prefer the one whose answer would most change the accepted outcome, meaning, or ability of the work. Do not choose a question because it is easy for the available tools, resembles a fashionable feature, or can produce an impressive side artifact. A generic opportunity list, repository summary, or restatement of the project's own documentation is not discovery.

Keep the distinction between project evidence and a proposed direction. Existing artifacts show what the project currently does or assumes; they do not automatically authorize a new product identity.

## Pursue it in the same commission

State the question precisely enough to know what a useful result would change, then do the work needed to reach that result now:

- reconcile material and develop the explanation when the deciding work is interpretive;
- investigate external facts when evidence outside the project could change the answer;
- develop a real candidate when the question concerns a creative possibility;
- make the difference judgeable when the question exposes a product or value choice;
- use the active domain method when the result requires implementation, measurement, or another specialized practice.

These are method choices inside one commission, not a mandatory pipeline. Do not stop at a backlog, research plan, proposed experiment, or recommendation to make a candidate later when the current authority and available material permit the work itself. If a real external gate blocks completion, return the strongest supported result and name the exact missing observation.

## Return the result to the project

Lead with what the pursued question changed or established. Give the explanation, probe result, candidate, correction, or other usable outcome at the fidelity the project needs. Explain why this question mattered only as much as needed to assess the result.

For a continuing project, update its existing record with the reason the question arose, evidence the result depends on, one useful unused clue, and the observation that would reopen the judgment. Do not create a second ledger or turn a provisional answer into the project's permanent identity.

A technically successful artifact is not automatically product progress. If it does not answer the project-grounded question or advance the accepted outcome, keep any independent learning at its proper scope and do not make the artifact representative of the work.
