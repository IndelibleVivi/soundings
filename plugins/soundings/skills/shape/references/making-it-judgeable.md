# Making it judgeable

Use this reference when a person cannot reasonably choose from abstract language alone, or when the agent must test a consequential interpretation before scaling it.

## Choose material that exposes the real difference

- Use a short recommendation when the trade-off is already concrete.
- Use a scenario when the distinction concerns what a person can do or feel over time.
- Use comparable samples when presentation, voice, composition, or interaction is the decision. Hold irrelevant variables and fidelity roughly constant.
- Use a representative output when a provider, export path, generator, or transformation will be judged by its visible result.
- Use a probe when actual behavior, performance, or interaction cannot be established by reading.
- Ask a focused question when the missing input is an owner-held value or preference that no artifact can answer for them.

Do not make the user supply professional terminology. Translate technical, vendor, or design differences into practical consequences they can recognize.

Use as few alternatives as expose the real decision. Prefer a current proposal and one genuinely different counterform when that is sufficient; do not multiply options merely to perform openness.

## Build enough, not automatically the least

The smallest artifact is useful only if it preserves the difference being tested. A broken interaction fragment, low-fidelity visual, or unrealistic sample may force the user to imagine away the very issue they are meant to judge.

Conversely, do not build a polished system around an unresolved assumption. Choose the narrowest coherent slice that exposes that assumption, while continuing unrelated safe work when appropriate.

If the user already authorized drafting, prototyping, comparing, or probing, deliver the judgeable slice in the current task. Do not hand back a proposal to make the slice later unless the real production exceeds the authorized scope or requires unavailable input.

## Keep the evidence scope visible

Describe the observation that would distinguish the alternatives before judging a probe. For a proposed “keep a chosen relationship while varying the rest” interaction, check both whether the relationship survives and whether the remaining variation is still useful. A static attractive image tests neither behavior by itself.

Interpret disappointing results at the layer actually exposed: mechanism, content, execution quality, learning cost, or compatibility with an accepted goal. Repair an inadequate sample when it has not tested the intended difference; change the mechanism when representative evidence challenges it. Do not keep building around a failed premise to protect sunk work.

Bring the observation back into the artifact and dependent decisions. A probe that produces insight but leaves the rejected assumption active has not completed a correction. Conversely, an observed local failure does not justify shrinking unrelated scope.

A preferred concept selects a direction for further development; it does not prove production quality. A successful technical probe establishes behavior; it does not establish that the user likes the result. A positive response to one component does not approve every adjacent choice.

State what the material can decide and what remains outside its scope only when confusion is likely; do not wrap every response in approval language.
