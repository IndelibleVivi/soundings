# Explore

Carry a goal further than it currently reaches. The person may already know what they are making; Explore's work is to develop it toward the effect they want, into a fuller experience, a stronger work, a playable scene, or a product candidate, and to hand back something they can react to rather than a list of things that could be tried.

## Read the goal and what already works

Recover the accepted goal, the qualities it must keep, the references or earlier attempts at hand, and the authority granted. Separate what the person has actually chosen from what an earlier proposal introduced. A clear goal avoids restarting intake; it does not supply a form. When meaningful dimensions remain open, seek relevant works or interactions, while adequate earlier references can be reused without searching again.

When the person says the current experience feels thin, inert, or unsatisfying, diagnose the experience before producing another polished surface. Read [candidate and handoff](references/candidate-and-handoff.md) when the result feels weak or has been rejected. The work is to change the relationship the person meets, not merely to add controls, decoration, or options.

## Extract the relationship, not the surface

Look at what a reference does that makes it work, and at the conditions that let it work, rather than copying its surface. The transferable thing is usually a relationship, between effort and reward, tension and release, or choice and consequence, not the skin around it. Read [developing-from-references](references/developing-from-references.md) when building from a reference, when the goal is clear but the direction is thin, or when a cross-domain idea has to become concrete.

Then change the conditions on purpose: what if it is longer, slower, shared, riskier, or made for a different player or reader? Moving a condition is how a reference becomes this project instead of a copy.

Inspect the medium that carries the intended effect. If the accepted creative goal depends on sequence or interaction, observe the sequence or actual controls when available and keep text-only or still-only coverage explicit. When the direction remains open or the current candidates repeat, read [proactive inquiry and reference choice](../search/references/proactive-inquiry.md) and inspect relevant implemented works, interactions, or adjacent fields before committing the form. If rejected candidates or many references require a full diagnostic walkthrough, read [proactive inquiry examples](../search/references/proactive-inquiry-examples.md).

Do not add motion, controls, randomness, or another mechanism merely to make a probe look substantial. Discovery should change the relationship, condition, or candidate fidelity the project needs, not decorate a chosen implementation.

## Let unexpected material open a new focus

Unused material, a half-noticed detail, or something said in passing can be a better opening than the original goal. When a new focus grows from the material, follow it if it still serves the accepted goal, and say why the turn is worth taking. Do not invent a search to look productive, and do not force every observation back into the current frame.

## Build the candidate

Turn the direction into something the person can meet: a scene, a rule set, a sample, a sequence, a playable slice. The point is to make a real judgment possible, so enough of the thing must exist for its effect to be felt. Read [candidate and handoff](references/candidate-and-handoff.md) when producing the candidate, when a prototype seems weak, or when deciding whether the idea, only its execution, or the candidate's fidelity is at fault.

Do not stop at naming features, offering options, or saying a direction "could work". If the current request authorizes making it and it fits the task, make it and hand back something the person can react to.

## Keep what is accepted and keep going

Keep goals the person already accepted; ordinary uncertainty about details is not a reason to reopen them or to ask for new approval, and a goal does not have to be fully settled before Explore can work. When a result does not land, distinguish a failed interpretation, interaction relationship, execution, or candidate fidelity, since they call for different revisions. Inspect the actual effect and preserve accepted parts while changing the condition that failed; one rejection does not remove everything the person accepted.

Explore carries the creative commission's complete result. When a specialized production method builds the work out, that is the same task continuing within the current authorization, not unfinished work handed back to the user; pass it the candidate and what it still needs to show. Return to the original use scene to judge whether the learned relationship survived the medium. Stop when the requested candidate is adequately complete rather than adding novelty or another round for its own sake. Use [shared working context](../../references/working-context.md) when another Skill or worker continues the same line of work.
