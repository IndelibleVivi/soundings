# Developing from references

Use this reference when a reference should change the work rather than be copied, when a clear goal is still too thin to build, or when an idea from another domain has to become concrete here. The examples below are constructed illustrations, not findings about real projects.

## Find the working relationship

A reference works through some relationship between its parts. Name it before borrowing anything:

- a resource that is scarce, then spent, then earned back;
- tension that builds toward a release the player controls;
- a small, frequent choice whose cost shows up later;
- a voice that stays slightly ahead of the reader.

Example: suppose a game grants the player one brief grace window after a mistake. The reference is not really about invincibility; the relationship is that failure is absorbed once and then charged. That relationship can move into a puzzle game's retry flow, where a wrong guess costs time instead of ending the run, so the surface is gone while the relationship survives.

When you cannot name the relationship, you are probably about to copy the surface. Keep looking, or choose a different reference.

## Change the conditions to make it yours

A copied reference reproduces its context; a developed one keeps the relationship and moves the conditions. Ask:

- what if this takes much longer, or resolves in one sitting?
- what if it is played alone instead of watched together?
- what if the stakes are reversible, or permanent?
- what if the audience is a beginner, an expert, or a stranger?

Each changed condition is a place the project can become its own thing. Say which condition you moved and what it bought, so the choice is visible rather than looking accidental.

## Move it across domains when it pays

A relationship that works elsewhere can be a strong source of directions, but you do not have to roam across unrelated references. Read widely when the current frame is thin or repetitive; stay with one well-understood reference when it already carries the effect you want. When you do transfer across domains, carry the relationship and name what must be true for it to work here.

Example: a board game's hidden-objective relationship, where each player knows something no one else does, can move into a paired reflection app as asymmetric prompts. The transfer is worth making because private knowledge changing what a person says is the mechanism, not the game.

## Keep the effect testable

Decide what a person should feel or be able to do once the relationship is in place, and let that decide how much to build. A relationship that cannot be felt in the candidate is only a slogan. The candidate is where the effect is tested; when a full version is impractical within the current scope, a slice that shows the relationship is one form of the work, not the endpoint of the commission.
